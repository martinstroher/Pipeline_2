## Two checks (run across the whole set)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. **Sibling near-synonym redundancy.** Do two (or more) terms denote the **same kind** — near-identical NLDs, or one is just a spelling/word-order/synonym variant of another (e.g. `Sofa` vs `Couch`)? Keep the clearest single survivor; mark the other(s) DROP_AS_REDUNDANT, `survivor` = the kept term. Judge by **meaning**, not surface wording — two genuinely distinct subkinds that merely sound similar must both be KEPT.

2. **Weak intermediate.** A term with `is_intermediate: true` earns its place only if it has **≥2 surviving children** AND a real differentia. Use the provided `child_count`. If `child_count < 2` (or it has no real differentia) → DROP_AS_REDUNDANT (its children auto-reparent to the category root). `survivor` = the category root or the term's parent.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
## Abstention
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- Default to **KEEP**. Drop when you can name the surviving term it collapses into (sibling) or cite `child_count < 2` (weak intermediate).
- Do NOT judge mixins, roles, instances, or parent-collapse here. Two terms that are both legitimate subkinds of the same parent are NOT redundant just because they share a parent.
- A term may appear only once in `edits`.
